What's new in Pulse

Pulse was upgraded in November 2026 in six phases, adding Jira's core work management to Omni Contact's project tracker. This guide explains each phase: what it added, what changed for you, and where to find it, with screenshots from the system.

The screenshots show demo data (names ending in "(demo)", and made-up clients and customers) on a test copy of Pulse. What you see depends on your role: the menus and pages only show what your role may use.

Before Phase 0A new database

Pulse's data moved from Cloudflare D1 to Postgres on Neon. You won't see a screen for this. It made the later phases possible (they need a database that handles many related records and reports), and every release since has been backed up first, with a restore point kept in Neon.

What changed for you: nothing to learn. Pulse kept every project, request, Concept Note, user and setting.

Phase 0Sign-in lockout

Pulse now stops people guessing passwords. Too many wrong passwords on one account within 15 minutes locks that account for a while; too many failed sign-ins from one internet address refuses that address. Both see the same message as a wrong password, so nobody can tell which usernames exist.

What changed for you: if you mistype your password several times, wait for the lock to end (15 minutes by default) or ask an administrator, who can unlock you at once in Settings > Administration > People. Locks and unlocks are in the activity log, and administrators are told under the bell.

Phase 1Work items

A project is now more than a progress bar. Its work is broken into work items, each with a key made of the project's key and a number (for example CCC-9), so anyone can refer to it in a message, a commit or a meeting.

  • Five types: Epic (a large piece of work), Story, Task, Bug, and Sub-task (part of a story, task or bug).
  • Each item has a summary, a description (with formatting), status, priority, assignee, reporter, labels, component, story points, dates and time estimates.
  • Collaboration: comments with @mentions, watchers, links between items ("blocks", "relates to"…), file attachments, time logs, and a full status history.
  • Feature requests are work items too: each client request appears both on the Feature requests page and as a work item.
What changed for you: projects have a Work items tab, and the Dashboard shows My work. Developers still see only the items assigned to them or that they reported, as with feature requests. A project can take its progress from its work items (the share that are done) instead of being updated by hand.

Phase 2Workflows and boards

Teams work differently, so statuses are no longer fixed. A workflow is the list of statuses a work item goes through and the moves allowed between them. Each move can have rules:

  • Conditions: who may make the move (for example, only a Manager may deploy).
  • Validators: what must be true first (a comment, an assignee, a filled-in field, finished sub-tasks).
  • Post-functions: what happens after (assign someone, set the resolution, notify people).

Each workspace uses a workflow, and a project can use a different one. Two are provided: Default (To Do, In Progress, In Review, Done) and Infrastructure deployment (with Testing and a Change Approval step).

Every project, and every workspace, has a Kanban board: a column per status (or group of statuses), with cards you move by dragging or with the Move menu.

What changed for you: moving an item goes through its workflow, so you may be asked for a comment or a field before a move is allowed. There's a new Boards page in the sidebar for workspace boards. WIP limits show in Settings > Administration > Workload limits.

Phase 3Sprints, timeline and reports

Sprints (Scrum)

A project can choose to plan in sprints (tick "Plan in sprints" on its Work items tab). It then gets a Backlog tab: the running and planned sprints at the top, the ranked backlog below. Drag items into a sprint (or use Move to…), type story points in place, then start the sprint. Completing it moves unfinished work to the backlog or the next sprint, and keeps a snapshot of what was committed, completed, added and removed.

Timeline

The Timeline tab shows epics and their items as bars between their start and due dates. Drag a bar to move it, or its ends to change dates; drag from a bar's dot onto another to say it blocks that one. Pulse warns when a blocker ends after the item it blocks starts.

Agile reports

Reports gained an Agile reports panel: burndown, burnup, velocity, sprint report, cumulative flow, control chart and lead and cycle time, for a project or a whole workspace. Each explains what to look for, and can be exported to CSV or printed.

What changed for you: Scrum is optional, project by project; Kanban projects carry on as before. Sprint health (days left, points done, behind or not) is in the Monday Briefing and Ask Pulse. "Plan and run sprints" is a new permission for Admins, Managers and Team Leaders.

Phase 4Search, dashboards and automation

Search and PQL

The new Search page finds work items across every project you can see. Use Basic mode (choose from lists) or PQL, Pulse's query language, which works like Jira's JQL:

project = CCC AND status != Done AND assignee = currentUser() ORDER BY priority DESC

Pulse suggests fields, values and functions as you type, and points at mistakes ("did you mean status?"). Results can be sorted and exported to CSV.

Saved filters and dashboards

Save any search as a filter, keep it private or share it with a role, a workspace or everyone. Filters feed dashboards: choose a dashboard at the top of the Dashboard page, or make your own with up to twenty gadgets in one to three columns. The eight gadget kinds are a list of items, pie chart, created vs resolved, sprint health, workload by person, overdue items, a two-way table, and recent activity. The original dashboard stays as Pulse overview, the default.

Automation

An automation rule does routine work: when something happens (an item is created, moves, changes, gets a comment, its sub-tasks are done, a sprint starts or ends, or at a set time), if it matches a PQL condition, then act (move it, assign it, set a field, comment, notify people, create a sub-task, link it). Three example rules come switched off.

What changed for you: new Search in the sidebar; a dashboard picker on the Dashboard; Ask Pulse can turn a question into a search ("Turn this into a search"). Changes made by a rule appear in history as "Automation: rule name". Sharing filters and dashboards, and managing automation, are new permissions.

Phase 5Integrations, administration and service desk

GitHub

Once GitHub is connected, commits, branches and pull requests that mention a work item's key (for example LP-1 in a commit message or a branch called feature/lp-1) appear in a Development panel on that item.

The API, tokens and webhooks

Other systems can use Pulse's API with a personal API token, made in Settings > Security & privacy. A token acts as you, with your permissions; it can be read-only or read-and-write, expires, is shown once, and can be revoked at any time. Administrators can see and revoke everyone's. Outbound webhooks tell another system when work items are created, changed, moved or commented on.

Notification schemes

Administrators decide, for each work item event, who is told (assignee, reporter, watchers, project lead, @mentioned people, everyone with a role) and how (bell, email, Teams). The defaults do what Pulse did before. Each person can still opt out of any event in Settings > Notifications.

Security levels

A work item can be marked confidential with a security level, such as "Confidential – Managers only" or "HR only". Only administrators, the item's assignee and reporter, and the roles and people the level names can see it, everywhere in Pulse (lists, boards, reports, search, dashboards, notifications, Ask Pulse, the API). View-only links never see these items.

Custom fields

Each workspace can have its own custom fields (text, number, date, single or multi select, person). Administrators choose where each appears (create form, item page, board cards) and whether it's required. Custom fields can be searched in PQL ("Client tier" = Gold), saved in filters and exported to CSV.

Service desk

Pulse now has a service desk for incidents, service requests, changes and questions, from staff and from people without a Pulse account.

  • The portal: a link (like a view-only link, expiring and revocable) for a client or site. People raise requests there and follow their own; they never see internal notes or other items. Staff raise requests from Service desk > Raise a request.
  • Queues: Unassigned, My open requests, SLA at risk, Breached and All open, most urgent first.
  • SLAs: first-response and resolution targets per request type and priority, counted in business hours (Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 17:00 Harare time, less public holidays). The clock stops while a request waits for the customer. Pulse warns at 75% of a target and alerts on a breach.
  • Replies: tick "Reply to the customer" on a comment and the customer sees it on the portal and by email; anything else is an internal note.
  • Changes must go through Change Approval, which a Manager approves.
What changed for you: new Service desk in the sidebar; new Settings pages for integrations, notification schemes, security levels, custom fields, the service desk and API tokens; "Reply to the customer" on service desk items; and an opt-out list in Settings > Notifications. Five new permissions control who can do what (see below).

Who can do what

Each phase added permissions. Administrators can change any of these in Settings > Administration > Roles & permissions; these are the defaults.

Anyone signed in can search, keep private filters and dashboards, and raise service desk requests. The rules that were already there still hold everywhere: developers see only their own items, feature requests and Concept Notes, and view-only links are read-only and never see contact details, documents or confidential items. Ask Pulse follows the same rules.
